# AI comparison (Workshop task 6)

Files in this folder: `prompt.txt` (exact prompt), `ai_dashboard.py` (code
generated by the AI), `ai_dashboard.png` (screenshot), and this comparison.

## 1. Tool used
Gemini (web app), used on 8 November 2026.

## 2. Prompt
The exact prompt is in `prompt.txt`. It gives the database schema and the
business goal (an airline operations team that wants to know where, when
and why flights are delayed or cancelled, with an IT head and a general
executive as audience). It does not give our team rules (delayed means
arrival delay of 15 minutes or more, cancelled and diverted flights are
excluded). This is deliberate: we wanted to see which choices the AI makes
on its own.

## 3. What an AI-generated dashboard is
A dashboard whose code, queries and charts are written by an AI model from
a description of the data and the goal. It is very fast, and it produced a
working first version in one answer. But the model does not know the
company's business rules, so its definitions and numbers have to be checked
by someone who knows the data.

## 4. What the AI produced
One Streamlit application with four KPIs (on-time rate, cancellation rate,
total delay time with an estimated cost, main delay cause), sidebar filters
by airline and month, and three tabs: why (delay causes and cancellation
reasons), where (a map of airports and a top 10 of routes) and when (delays
by hour and a day-by-hour heatmap).

## 5. How we checked it
We ran its dashboard on our database, with no filter selected, and compared
what it shows with the rules and numbers of our own analysis.

| Check | AI dashboard | Our dashboard |
|---|---|---|
| Flights used for the rates | All rows loaded, including cancelled and diverted flights | 49,139 in-scope flights (cancelled and diverted excluded) |
| Definition of on time / delayed | On time = arrival delay <= 15 min. Total delay = every positive delay, even 1 minute | Delayed = arrival delay >= 15 min |
| On-time rate | 80.7 % | 81.2 % |
| Total delay minutes | 599,538 | 543,220, equal to the sum of the five causes |
| Delay causes | Same totals as ours (212,314 late aircraft, 175,521 airline, 125,443 air system, 29,372 weather, 570 security), summed in Python | `delay_causes` table, checked against the arrival delay totals |
| Where the calculations happen | In pandas, after loading every flight row | In SQL views, the dashboard only displays them |

The two total delay figures differ by 56,318 minutes. They are the delays of
1 to 14 minutes, which the AI counts as delay and we do not. The AI page
therefore shows two different totals for the same thing: 599,538 minutes in
the KPI card and 543,220 minutes in the sum of its own cause chart.

## 6. Critical comparison

| Criterion | AI dashboard | Our dashboard |
|---|---|---|
| Analytical relevance | Good: its why / where / when structure matches our questions and covers cancellations too. | Each theme answers one question and ends with a finding and a recommendation. |
| KPI selection | Sensible list, but it adds an invented 80% target and a cost estimate (74 dollars per minute, about 44 million in total) that comes from nowhere in our data. | KPIs come only from our data and the team's definitions. |
| Visual clarity | More variety (map, heatmap, tabs, filters). On our screen its titles and KPI labels are almost invisible, because its custom light background clashes with Streamlit's dark theme. The hourly curve and the heatmap show gaps and spikes in the early hours, probably from very few flights. | Focused on one question: filters by airline and month, a bubble chart that separates frequent causes from severe ones, fixed colors per cause, and text computed from the data. |
| Correctness | Its numbers do not match ours: different denominator, a different threshold, and two definitions of delay inside the same page. Its top 10 routes is led by routes with a single flight (for example MQT to DTW, 1 flight, 433 minutes), because there is no minimum number of flights. Airports with numeric codes disappear silently from the map and routes. | Numbers reconcile across the dashboard (the check is displayed on the page), the causes add up to the arrival delay, and the team rules (minimum of 30 flights, exclusions) are applied. |
| Decision-support value | Good for exploring, but an executive could quote its cost figure or its on-time rate without knowing how they are defined. | Every number can be traced to a SQL view, and a warning appears when a selection has fewer than 30 delayed flights. |

## 7. What the AI did well, and what we had to fix, check or redesign

**Done well.** A complete, runnable application in one answer, with a
structure that fits the audience. Sidebar filters, a map, a day-by-hour
heatmap, readable cancellation reasons through a join with the reasons
table, and a minimum volume for the airport map. The filters and the
interactivity inspired the filters we added to our own page.

**Fixed.** We did not change the generated code. We only had to configure
it: it reads the connection settings from environment variables and assumes
a database named `airline_db`, so we set the variables for our own
database, `flights_db`, before running it.

**Checked and found wrong or doubtful.**
- On-time rate and total delay use different thresholds and a different
  population than the team's rules, so they do not match our figures.
- The cost per minute is a made-up benchmark.
- The text says the SQL engine does the aggregations, but the code loads
  every row and aggregates in pandas. It also does not use our
  `delay_causes` table.
- The route top 10 is dominated by routes with one to three flights.
- Flights with numeric airport codes (about 8% of the sample) are dropped
  silently from the map and the routes.

**What we redesigned or borrowed.** We kept our SQL views as the source of
the numbers and added airline and month filters, which the AI did well.
The filters work on small pre-aggregated views (at most 840 rows) so the
logic stays in SQL. Any AI generated dashboard must be checked against the
business rules before it is shown to decision-makers.
